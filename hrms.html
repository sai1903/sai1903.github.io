Panels that stop being a calendar problem

The scheduler reads interviewer load, timezone and the panel composition rules you set — no two juniors on a senior loop, no one doing four interviews in a day. Scorecards are locked per interviewer until everyone submits, which kills the anchoring problem.

  • Structured scorecards per role, versioned
  • F2F, virtual and panel rounds with room booking
  • Blind-review mode for the first screen
  • Talent pool that remembers the near-misses

The offer letter generates itself

Grade, location and entity decide the template. Finance approves the number, not the wording. Once the candidate signs, the record flips to employee and onboarding starts firing — accounts, device, documents and day-one plan, all keyed off the join date.

  • Templated letters per entity, legal-approved once
  • Approval chain with budget check before send
  • E-signature and counter-signature, stored with the record
  • Background check and document collection tracked to completion

Payroll that an auditor can read

Each run is immutable once approved. Corrections become arrears in the next cycle rather than edits to a closed month, so the ledger and the payslips never disagree. Statutory components are calculated per entity and filed on a schedule.

  • PF, ESI, professional tax and TDS per entity
  • Proration, arrears, mid-month joins and exits handled
  • Leave and attendance feed the run directly
  • Full & final settlement generated at offboarding

Reviews tied to what people actually did

Goals set at the start of a cycle carry through to the review form, so nobody reconstructs a year from memory the night before. Calibration happens on a grid the whole management line can see, and compensation changes write straight to payroll.

  • Self, manager, peer and skip-level inputs in one cycle
  • Calibration grid with distribution warnings, not hard quotas
  • Learning paths assigned from review outcomes
  • Compliance training with expiry and re-assignment
